A sentence beats another chart

What changed, and what is uncertain

Placements reached 214 against a 300 target, ahead of the linear target path but decelerating sharply in July (+7, against +29 in June). The slowdown traces to a single bottleneck: median time from partner confirmation to the youth actually presenting is 52 days, and 145 youth are sitting in that gap.

Livelihood grant disbursement is the weakest indicator at 37% of target. The business plans exist; the transfers have not followed. This mirrors the target-versus-actual shortfall pattern in the parent PSNP 5 operation and should be treated as a financing-process problem, not a demand problem.

Female placement rate is 8 percentage points below male (21% vs 29%) on stable denominators. Worth investigating; the disability disaggregation (14%, n = 28) is too small to say anything about and should not be reported as a finding.

Three caveats on these numbers. Placement counts are gross, not net of deadweight or displacement — they are not "jobs created". 41 of the 214 outcomes are self-reported and not yet employer-confirmed; the verified subset is 173. And with roughly 87% of Ethiopian youth employment being informal, a formal-wage-only reading of these figures will understate what the pilot actually achieved.

Variables and visualisation choices

Why each variable sits where it does, and why each chart is the chart it is

This replaces Section 8 of the Development Specification, which lists nine dashboards in one flat table with no audience split, no indicator definitions, and no visualisation guidance. The nine are all still here — they are distributed across four audiences and given formulas, denominators and chart types.

The organising decision. Build four small dashboards, not one dashboard with role-based hiding. A single dashboard with permissions always converges on the union of every stakeholder's requirements, and the case manager ends up looking at donor indicators. Four dashboards, six cards each, hard cap nine.

4. The rules that stop the dashboard lying

Minimum denominator — three bands, enforced once in SQL

BandConditionDisplay
Reportdenominator ≥ 30Rate, plus n/N counts, plus 95% CI where it matters
Provisional10 ≤ denominator < 30Rate with a persistent visible marker (52%*) and a footnote. Never used in a comparison or a ranking.
Suppressdenominator < 10— and "too few to assess"
  • Never show a percentage without its denominator. Not on a tile, not in a tooltip, not in the donor PDF.
  • Apply the rule to every disaggregation, not just the headline. Female × PWD × Woreda C is exactly where denominators collapse, and it is exactly what donors ask for.
  • Suppression must be non-complementary. Suppress one cell in a row and publish the total, and the hidden value is recoverable by subtraction. Suppress a second.
  • Distinguish "0" from "no data". Zero placements from 40 referrals and no report submitted are completely different findings and must look different on screen.
  • Whole percentage points only. 49%, never 48.81%.

Denominator maturation

Every checkpoint metric needs an explicit eligible denominator. A youth placed 20 days ago is not a 30-day retention failure, and a referral raised three days before period end is not an unclosed loop. Without maturation windows, rates collapse at every period boundary and staff learn to stop raising referrals late in the quarter.

Vanity metrics to keep off every screen

  • "Referrals made." The textbook vanity metric for a referral platform. A referral costs nothing and produces nothing. Count confirmed, attended, outcome recorded.
  • Placement rate shown to a case manager. Creates cream-skimming pressure with no compensating information value.
  • Raw placement-rate leaderboards across woredas. Programmes serving harder cases report lower placement rates. An unadjusted leaderboard rewards creaming and penalises the woreda doing the difficult work. Show intake vulnerability profile alongside, or do not rank.
  • Real-time refresh on the donor dashboard. Freeze it monthly with a visible as-of date.

Attribution rules to settle before Sprint 7

  • A youth referred to three partners and placed via one: whose numerator? Decide, document it, and put the rule in the card subtitle.
  • Deduplicate at person level within a reporting period, or a youth with three referrals inflates counts threefold.
  • Carry a verification_source field on every outcome (self-reported / employer-confirmed / document-verified / provider-confirmed) and let the dashboard filter on it. Report the verified subset as the headline.
  • Label placements gross. They are not "jobs created" — that claim requires netting out deadweight and displacement, which this platform cannot do.

5. Colour, accessibility and bandwidth

Series colours use a machine-validated palette. Categorical #12836B · #D08A0A · #B0442A · #7A4CA8 and the sequential ramp #84BEAC → #0C5346 both pass lightness-band, chroma-floor, colour-vision-deficiency separation, normal-vision separation and surface-contrast checks. The gold sits below 3:1 against the page, which is why every gold segment on this prototype carries a visible label rather than relying on fill alone.

  • Never colour alone. Every status pairs a colour with a word and a geometric mark — this survives monochrome screens, colour-blind readers, and a phone in direct sunlight.
  • Every chart has a table twin. That is the WCAG 1.1.1 text alternative and the fallback when SVG fails on a low-end browser. It is also what users actually read.
  • Adjacent chart segments need 3:1 contrast against each other (WCAG 1.4.11). This is what kills the six-segment stacked bar, not aesthetics.
  • Sunlight: no shadows, no gradients, no light-grey secondary text, minimum 2px strokes, weight 600+ on any number that matters.
  • Bandwidth: chart type barely affects page weight. What matters is card count (each is a query round-trip — cap at 6, hard cap 9), result-set size (aggregate in SQL, never return detail rows), and external requests (which is where maps lose badly).
  • Pre-aggregate. Materialised views refreshed nightly — mv_funnel_daily, mv_referral_outcomes, mv_cohort_retention — not five-CTE queries run live on every dashboard load.
  • Server-rendered email beats an interactive dashboard on 3G. A 07:00 snapshot in the supervisor's inbox works when the network does not.

6. Two architecture calls this implies for the Dev Spec

The case manager dashboard is not a Metabase dashboard. It belongs in Django, inside the case management app. It needs per-user row filtering, sub-second response on 3G, and an action affordance next to every number. Metabase covers tiers 2, 3 and 4. Relying on Metabase row-level security for per-youth PII isolation puts your data-protection boundary in a paid BI feature — that boundary belongs in the Django ORM, enforced and tested.
Sprint 7 needs to move earlier, and split. Dev Spec Section 8 puts all nine dashboards in one late sprint. The case manager work queue is not a reporting feature — it is the thing that makes the system worth opening, and it should ship with the referral engine in Sprint 3–4. Only tiers 2 to 4 belong in Sprint 7.

7. What still needs a decision

  • Retention convention: 30/60/90 days from placement for operations, 3/6/12 months from programme exit for reporting. Both are implemented here; the donor-facing anchor needs task-team sign-off.
  • Whether subsidised placements (PSNP public works) count in the employment numerator. They should not, but that needs stating.
  • The caseload ceiling and stall threshold defaults, which drive half the alerts on the case manager screen.
  • Whether the suppression thresholds above (30 / 10) are accepted, or the World Bank task team prescribes its own.
  • Metabase's rendering of Amharic and Afaan Oromo in card titles, axis labels and exports — verify before committing, not after.
